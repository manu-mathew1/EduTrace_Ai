### Question 1 (10 Marks) — The Keynesian Multiplier with Tax Leakage
Consider a closed economy described by the following behavioral equations:
- Consumption: $C = 300 + 0.8(Y - T)$
- Investment: $I = 200$
- Government Purchases: $G = 250$
- Taxes: $T = 0.25 Y$

#### Tasks:
1. **Derive the equilibrium output $Y^*$ algebraically.**
2. **Calculate the government purchases multiplier $\frac{\Delta Y}{\Delta G}$.**
3. **A policy aide calculates the multiplier as $\frac{1}{1 - 0.8} = 5.0$. Explain why this calculation is incorrect and quantify the magnitude of the estimation error.**

#### Official Marking Rubric:
* **Rubric Step 1 (2 Marks):** Substitute disposable income $Y_d = (1 - 0.25)Y = 0.75Y$ into consumption function: $C = 300 + 0.6Y$.
* **Rubric Step 2 (3 Marks):** Set $Y = C + I + G \implies Y = 300 + 0.6Y + 200 + 250 = 750 + 0.6Y$.
* **Rubric Step 3 (3 Marks):** Solve for $Y^* = \frac{750}{1 - 0.6} = \frac{750}{0.4} = 1875$.
* **Rubric Step 4 (2 Marks):** Compute multiplier $k = \frac{1}{1 - 0.6} = 2.5$. Identify error: aide omitted the tax rate $t = 0.25$, doubling the true stimulus effect ($5.0$ vs $2.5$).

### Question 2 (10 Marks) — IS-LM Equilibrium & Crowding-Out
Suppose the goods market is characterized by $Y = C(Y - T) + I(r) + G$, and the money market by $\frac{M}{P} = L(r, Y)$.
Suppose the government increases purchases by $\Delta G = 100$.

#### Tasks:
1. **Explain why output does not increase by the full Keynesian multiplier in the presence of an upward-sloping LM curve.**
2. **Describe step-by-step how the increase in government spending leads to crowding-out of private investment.**

#### Official Marking Rubric:
* **Rubric Step 1 (3 Marks):** IS curve shifts rightward by $k \cdot \Delta G$.
* **Rubric Step 2 (4 Marks):** Higher income increases money demand ($L(r, Y) \uparrow$); with fixed money supply, interest rate must rise ($r \uparrow$).
* **Rubric Step 3 (3 Marks):** Higher interest rate dampens interest-sensitive investment $I(r) \downarrow$, partially offsetting output expansion.

### Question 3 (10 Marks) — Supply-Side Shocks & Stagflation Dilemma
Using the short-run Aggregate Demand / Aggregate Supply (AS-AD) model, analyze the economic consequences of a sudden doubling in global crude oil prices.

#### Official Marking Rubric:
* **Rubric Step 1 (3 Marks):** Adverse supply shock shifts SRAS upward/leftward due to increased production costs.
* **Rubric Step 2 (4 Marks):** New short-run equilibrium features higher price level $P \uparrow$ and lower real output $Y \downarrow$ (Stagflation).
* **Rubric Step 3 (3 Marks):** Contrast monetary policy options: expansion cures unemployment but exacerbates inflation; contraction cures inflation but deepens recession.
